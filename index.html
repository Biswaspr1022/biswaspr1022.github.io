📖 What This Toolkit Does

This is a desktop companion made for players who want a calm, organized way to plan their Don't Starve Together sessions. Instead of keeping notes in your head while the seasons change around you, you get one place to record survivor builds, plan seasonal preparation, track crafting projects, set resource goals, and map out boss encounters with your friends.

The toolkit was built around a simple idea: most survival failures start long before the hounds arrive. They start when nobody remembered the winter gear, when the drying racks were never built, or when the group wandered into a boss fight with three torches and no plan. This companion keeps all of that visible so your team can move through autumn, winter, spring, and summer without scrambling.

It also includes multiplayer survival analytics, which simply means the tool keeps a record of how your group has been progressing over time. You can look back at previous plans, compare how different seasons went, and see which projects kept slipping. Everything is stored on your own PC.

✨ Main Features

  • Survivor builds planner — record the character builds your group wants to run, note the key items for each one, and keep everyone working toward the same setup.
  • Seasonal preparation checklists — separate checklists for autumn, winter, spring, and summer so nothing important is forgotten before the season flips.
  • Crafting project tracker — break large crafting goals into smaller steps and mark them off as your team gathers materials.
  • Resource goals — set target amounts for wood, stone, gold, silk, and other materials, then watch the numbers fill in as your group farms.
  • Boss planning boards — plan which bosses you want to take on, what gear each player needs, and what role everyone covers during the fight.
  • Multiplayer survival analytics — a simple overview of how your group's plans have gone over multiple sessions, so you can spot repeated trouble areas.
  • Local, private records — your plans are saved on your own computer and stay with you between sessions.

🧭 A Simple First Plan

If you are not sure where to begin, this short walkthrough gives you a useful plan in a few minutes. It also shows how the different parts of the toolkit connect to each other.

  1. Create a new plan and give it a name your group will recognize, such as the season and the year.
  2. Add your survivor builds. Note which character each player is using and the handful of items that build depends on.
  3. Open the seasonal checklist for the season you are heading into and tick off the preparation items as you complete them.
  4. Set three resource goals — the materials you always seem to run short of. Keeping the list short makes it far easier to actually finish.
  5. Add one crafting project and break it into small steps so it can be worked on between other tasks.
  6. Add a boss planning entry for the next boss your group wants to attempt, listing what each player needs to bring.

No additional software is needed. The toolkit is self-contained, so you can download it and use it without installing extra components.

Where are my plans stored

Plans are saved locally on your own computer alongside the application. They remain available the next time you open the toolkit and are not uploaded anywhere.

Can several players use it for the same group

Yes. Each player can keep their own copy of the toolkit and describe the same group plan from their own point of view, which is a good way to catch gaps in preparation before a season begins.

How do I move my plans to another computer

Copy the folder that contains the application and its saved data to a USB drive, then place it on the other computer. Since everything is stored locally, the plans travel with the folder.

💡 Tips for Better Seasons

  • Keep seasonal checklists short and specific. A list of five items people actually finish beats a list of twenty that gets ignored.
  • Update your resource goals at the end of each session while the numbers are still fresh in everyone's mind.
  • Write boss plans in the order you will do them, with the gear requirements first. It makes the preparation feel far less overwhelming.
  • Use the analytics view once every few sessions to see which projects keep sliding. That is usually where the real bottleneck is.
  • Give each plan a clear name. Months later, a name like the season and year will save you a lot of scrolling.
